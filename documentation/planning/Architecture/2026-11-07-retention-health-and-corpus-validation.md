# Retention, recording health and public-corpus validation

Date: 2026-11-07. The Claude Opus and Codex design reviews were already discussed and accepted. This increment continues that full design; no new preimplementation approval or reduced roadmap is implied. Final implementation review and release verification are recorded below when complete.

## Implemented behavior

- Local storage controls preview exact owned file/byte/run counts before manual confirmation. Whole-meeting deletion removes owned descendants; audio-only purging preserves transcripts, notes, speaker annotations, summaries, citations and search. Exported and backed-up copies remain outside deletion.
- Confirmed durable intents, capability-relative same-volume moves, transaction-scoped immutable DELETE gates, rollback/restoration and postcommit cleanup support crash recovery. Pending moves block every saved-audio/recovery path before ready chunks could be mistaken for missing audio. Startup resolves confirmed intents before ordinary recording recovery.
- Audio defaults to keep. Unreferenced obsolete transcript runs default to seven days, with an independent keep-all switch. Active/running, recent and summary/citation/audit/correction referenced runs remain protected. The desktop alone starts a weak background scheduler: 60-second checks, automatic attempts at least an hour apart even on failure, idle only and deferred during saved listening.
- Maintenance shares capture/processing exclusion and drops output before confirmed removal. Waveform scans use a reader lease, allowing snapshots while blocking destructive maintenance. Filesystem-heavy IPC and device inventory run outside the frontend thread. Device discovery no longer gates the first library snapshot.
- Source timing uses a validated Mach/raw-uptime common origin from protocol 3. Older/invalid helpers retain explicit observed-audio fallback. Notes attach provenance atomically with searchable text; saved notes without matching playback are explicitly untimed.
- Disk checks warn below decimal 2 GB, suspend inference below 500 MB and request clean capture stop below 100 MB. Per-chunk budget checks cover consumption between periodic checks. Tests inject readings instead of filling disks.
- Native relay packets are split to at most 20 ms before bounded queues without changing source PCM/frame/host positions. Staged shedding based on measured global backlog over ten seconds is still open.

Feature ownership: [retention](../../features/storage/retention/retention.md), [recording health](../../features/capture/recording-health/recording-health.md), [desktop integration](../../features/app/desktop-session/desktop-session.md), and [production inference](../../features/quality/production-inference/production-inference.md). Their baselines and existing unmerged trails describe current behavior.

## Public evidence and its limits

The optional `xtask --features inference` runner executes actual pinned production engines against explicitly supplied public files without microphone/output devices, application data or cloud calls. It preserves hashes, licence/source attribution, real hypotheses and scorer manifests. Model setup and corpus acquisition remain separate explicit actions.

The preserved first 300 seconds of AMI ES2002a Array1-01 contain four annotated speakers. The initial production run emitted no live transcript: WER 100%. Batch WER was 54.09%; live DER 56.74% and refined DER 54.54% under the recorded transcriber-segment reference protocol. Sampled process RSS reached about 1.44 GB across sequential phases. These speech scores do not establish acceptable quality. The process peak exceeds 1.2 GB, but sequential phases are not a qualified measurement of the live resource budget. Quiet far-field audio is a measured diagnostic factor; a separate public speech control produced text through the same runner. Fixed gain alone did not establish an acceptable solution.

The baseline reference includes transcriber vocal-event regions. Secondary standard `only_words` references and candidate-model diagnostics are separately labelled; they cannot replace the original baseline. The next 300 seconds have three lexical speakers, with a fourth present in vocal-event annotations. Any four-speaker holdout claim must identify its reference protocol. This public corpus does not satisfy the separate in-house four-speaker, gallery-of-50, long-call or reference-hardware gates. Candidate experiments do not activate new models, fixed gain or different production defaults.

Bounded candidate measurements preserve the same development and holdout audio. June 21 Zipformer raw WER was 52.33%/50.19%; fixed-gain variants improved further but remain experimental. Nemotron raw WER was 49.42%/48.25%, with substantially higher runtime and memory than the June 21 candidate. Under identical production speaker policies, a temporary offline-context-only change from five to ten seconds improved legacy-reference DER from 54.54% to 46.13% on development audio and 68.81% to 50.70% on the holdout. Secondary standard word-only reference scores are separately recorded. These experiments justify follow-up validation, without changing the installed model or production diarization policy.

See compact measurement snapshots under the production-inference feature for exact scores, derivation and provenance. Large audio/model/transcript artifacts stay in the temporary public-evidence cache rather than this repository.
